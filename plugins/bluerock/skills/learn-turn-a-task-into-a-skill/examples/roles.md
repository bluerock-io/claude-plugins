# Session 5 — role example blocks

The lesson is identical for everyone. Role matters more here than in any earlier
session for one reason: **step 3 is the session's likeliest stall**, and a builder
who can't name a workflow needs prompts from their own week, not generic ones.

Read `role` from `learning/progress.json` (`sales`, `marketing`, `operations`);
if unset, use the neutral block.

**Use these as prompts, never as a menu.** A workflow they picked off a list
without recognizing it as theirs produces a skill they never run, and step 6's
"real input" becomes a toy. The test is always: *have you already explained this
one to the AI at least twice?* If the answer is no, keep looking.

## sales

- **Candidate workflows:** the follow-up you send after a first call (different
  from `meeting-recap` — this one is your own shape); the weekly pipeline update
  to your manager; the re-engagement note to a deal that went quiet; the
  internal handoff when a deal moves to onboarding; turning a discovery call into
  a one-page account summary.
- **The one they usually pick:** the pipeline update. It's weekly, it's text in
  and text out, and they resent it last Friday.
- **The "what did you dread" prompt:** "what did you put off until Friday
  afternoon last week?"
- **Never-do rule to expect in their design:** don't invent commitments the
  customer didn't make. Sales builders find this one fast, and it's a good sign.
- **Real input for step 6:** an actual deal or account from this week, named.

## marketing

- **Candidate workflows:** turning a long piece into social posts; the campaign
  brief you fill in every launch; a first-draft of the monthly newsletter from
  the month's material; competitor-page notes into a positioning summary; the
  performance recap you write for a stakeholder who doesn't read dashboards.
- **The one they usually pick:** repurposing one asset into channel posts, since
  it's the most repeated and the most tedious.
- **The "what did you dread" prompt:** "what do you rewrite every time even
  though it's the same shape?"
- **Never-do rule to expect in their design:** don't invent statistics, and
  don't claim a result the data doesn't show. If they built a messaging doc in
  Session 2, point their skill at it — the description can say to read it, and
  their drafts get their brand's phrases for free.
- **Real input for step 6:** a real post or campaign from this week.

## operations

- **Candidate workflows:** the weekly status roll-up; a request-intake triage
  note; turning a process discussion into written SOP steps; the incident or
  escalation summary; the recurring report you reformat every month.
- **The one they usually pick:** the weekly status roll-up — and it pairs
  naturally with what `scribe` has been filing since Session 3, which is worth
  pointing out.
- **The "what did you dread" prompt:** "what do you assemble by hand every week
  from things you already wrote down?"
- **Never-do rule to expect in their design:** don't report a status you can't
  source, and don't smooth over a blocker. Ops builders write the sharpest
  never-do rules in the program; let them.
- **Real input for step 6:** this week's actual status, from their real notes.
  (Revenue and marketing ops: same block, adapt in one line.)

## neutral (role unset)

- **Candidate workflows:** a recurring email you send in roughly the same shape;
  a weekly update to someone who needs to know what happened; notes into a
  summary someone else reads; a document you reformat on a schedule.
- **The "what did you dread" prompt:** "what did you do last Friday afternoon
  that you'll do again this Friday?"
- **Second fallback:** "what would you hand a new hire first, if you had to
  write it down?" A workflow they could explain to a person is a workflow they
  can explain to a skill.
- **Never-do rule to expect in their design:** whatever they'd be embarrassed to
  send. That question — "what would be bad if it made this up?" — gets a real
  never-do rule out of anyone.
- **Real input for step 6:** anything real from this week, however small.
