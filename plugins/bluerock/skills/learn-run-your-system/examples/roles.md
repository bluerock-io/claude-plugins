# Session 8 — role example blocks

The capstone is identical for everyone: read the system back, say what it runs,
walk it if they can, find the gap, close out. Role changes the workflow statement
worth reaching for, who to present to, and which gaps to probe for in step 4.

Read `role` from `learning/progress.json` (`sales`, `marketing`, `operations`);
if unset, use the neutral block.

## sales

- **Workflow statements that usually land:** the morning brief that closes
  yesterday's commitments before the first call; notes-to-follow-up on the same
  day instead of Friday; the account read that used to take twenty minutes before
  a call.
- **The third sentence, their version:** "I stopped rebuilding my week from my
  calendar and my sent folder." "I stopped sending follow-ups two days late."
- **Who to present to:** their manager, or the AE or rep who keeps asking how
  they're turning follow-ups around so fast. A sales team meeting is the highest-
  leverage audience in the program — it's the one where someone else asks for it
  next.
- **Gaps to probe in step 4:** does anything check whether the follow-up actually
  went out? Is the account research still manual? What do they still retype into
  the CRM by hand?

## marketing

- **Workflow statements that usually land:** the competitive sweep that runs
  weekly instead of never; drafts that come back in their voice instead of needing
  a rewrite; the messaging doc that every later draft leans on.
- **The third sentence, their version:** "I stopped rewriting AI drafts to sound
  like me." "I stopped finding out about a competitor's launch from a colleague."
- **Who to present to:** their content lead, the founder who rewrites everyone's
  copy, or whoever guards the brand voice — the same person from Session 2's
  share moment, which makes a nice closing loop if they remember it.
- **Gaps to probe in step 4:** is the messaging doc still current, or has it aged?
  Does anything read performance data, or is that still by hand? Which channel do
  they still write from scratch every time?

## operations

- **Workflow statements that usually land:** the status roll-up assembled from
  filed notes instead of memory; the process doc that got written because a
  checker agent found what was ambiguous; the monthly report that drafts itself.
- **The third sentence, their version:** "I stopped assembling the weekly update
  from six different places." "I stopped being the only person who knew how this
  ran."
- **Who to present to:** their team, in a standup or a retro. Ops builders get the
  most from a live audience because their colleagues immediately want the same
  thing — and the requests they get *are* the gap list.
- **Gaps to probe in step 4:** what's still copied between systems by hand? Which
  recurring thing has no routine yet? What would break if they were out for a
  week — and does anything write that down? (Revenue and marketing ops: same
  block, adapt in one line.)

## neutral (role unset)

- **Workflow statements that usually land:** anything with a real trigger and a
  real destination. The morning brief is the most common and it's a good one.
- **The third sentence:** push for something concrete they no longer do. That's
  the sentence that makes the whole statement land, for them and for anyone they
  show it to.
- **Who to present to:** one colleague who has asked how they've been getting
  through things faster. If nobody comes to mind, a screen recording they watch
  back once works — the hesitation is the signal either way.
- **Gaps to probe in step 4:** what did they build and then never run again? What
  do they still do by hand every week? Which part would they least like to
  explain?
