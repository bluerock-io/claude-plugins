# Session 3 — role example blocks

The lesson is identical for everyone: run scribe, open its file and name the five
parts, add one section, run it on a real day. Role only changes the demo input and
which section they add in step 2. Read `role` from `learning/progress.json`
(`sales`, `marketing`, `operations`); if it's unset, use the neutral block and
don't stall the session to ask.

**The Wins section is the default for every role, and it's a good default** — it
is small, it lands in one line, and it gives them something pleasant to write in
step 3. The alternatives below are for a builder who wants the edit to be
theirs. Any of them works; only one section gets added.

## sales

- **Framing:** "You already keep this in your head after every call. scribe puts
  it in a file, in the same shape every time, so a week of calls is something you
  can actually read back."
- **Demo input for step 1:** the shipped one works as-is — a renewal call, a
  pricing commitment, a legal thread is a normal sales day. Or theirs: "call with
  [account] about [thing], they want [X] by [day], still waiting on [whoever]."
- **Section to add in step 2:** Wins. Alternative: **"Objections — what they
  pushed back on"**, which is the one most sales builders wish they'd tracked.
- **What to point at in the five parts:** Tools. scribe can't reach the web or
  their CRM — it files what they tell it, nothing more. That boundary is the
  answer to the question they're about to ask.
- **Step 3 nudge:** one real call from today, plus one thing that went well.

## marketing

- **Framing:** "The half-ideas you lose between meetings are the expensive ones.
  scribe catches them in the same place every day, in a shape you can mine
  later."
- **Demo input for step 1:** the shipped one is fine, or theirs: "reviewed the
  campaign draft with [person], decided to cut [thing], still waiting on
  [asset]."
- **Section to add in step 2:** Wins. Alternative: **"Ideas — anything worth
  coming back to"**, which pairs with the Brain dump section already in the
  template.
- **What to point at in the five parts:** Identity. One line — *a fast, quiet
  archivist* — is why it doesn't chat back at them. Voice in a spec is not
  decoration; it produced the behavior they just watched.
- **Step 3 nudge:** one real thing from today, plus one thing that went well.

## operations

- **Framing:** "Most of what you'd want in a weekly update is already in your
  head by 6pm and gone by Monday. scribe is where it goes instead, and it never
  edits yesterday."
- **Demo input for step 1:** the shipped one is fine, or theirs: "synced with
  [team] on [process], agreed to [change] by [day], blocked on [whoever]."
- **Section to add in step 2:** Wins. Alternative: **"Blockers — what stopped
  someone today"**, which is the one that pays off in a weekly review.
- **What to point at in the five parts:** Output. Append-only and today-only is
  why it can never quietly rewrite a record. Ops builders tend to care about
  this line more than anyone, and it is the honest answer to "can I trust it
  with my notes."
- **Step 3 nudge:** one real thing from today, plus one thing that went well.
  (Revenue and marketing ops: same block, adapt the examples in one line.)

## neutral (role unset)

- **Framing:** "An agent that files your day into the same place, in the same
  shape, every time. You're about to run it, then open the file that makes it
  behave that way."
- **Demo input for step 1:** the shipped one, as written.
- **Section to add in step 2:** Wins.
- **What to point at in the five parts:** Tools and Output together — what it's
  allowed to touch, and what it must never do. That pair is where an agent is
  kept on the rails, and it's the note the rest of the path builds on.
- **Step 3 nudge:** one real thing from today, plus one thing that went well.
