# Session 4 — role example blocks

The lesson is identical for everyone: let onboard draft the three files, sharpen
`voice.md` with a real rule and a real quote, prove it with a clean draft,
commit. Role only changes the examples and the proof draft. Read `role` from
`learning/progress.json` (`sales`, `marketing`, `operations`); if it's unset,
onboard is about to write "Who I am" into `CLAUDE.md` — read it there and
confirm in one line rather than asking cold. If you still can't tell, use the
neutral block.

Each block gives four things: how to frame the session, what writing samples to
ask for, prompts for the avoid-list when they blank (the common stall), and the
two-line draft to prove it with.

## sales

- **Framing:** "Every follow-up, every recap, every note you send is writing.
  Get your voice into one file and you stop rewriting AI drafts to sound like
  you — which is where most of the time goes."
- **Writing samples to ask for:** a follow-up email after a good call; a
  prospecting note that got a reply. Real ones that worked, not templates.
- **Avoid-list prompts when they stall:** the opener you delete from every AI
  draft ("I hope this email finds you well"); the word that makes you sound like
  a vendor instead of a person ("solution", "synergy", "touch base"); the
  exclamation point you never use.
- **The proof draft:** a two-line note declining a meeting. If they'd rather it
  be closer to the job, a two-line follow-up to a call that went fine but
  didn't move — same length, same test.
- **Objectives note:** rank by pipeline reality — the accounts and the quarter's
  number, not aspirations. `daily-brew` reads this to decide what counts as
  focus.

## marketing

- **Framing:** "You already have a brand voice doc somewhere. This is the
  version an agent can actually follow — rules and real lines instead of
  adjectives. It's also the file that stops every draft from reading like
  generic AI." If they built a messaging doc in Session 2, connect the two:
  that one is how the *brand* sounds; this one is how *they* write.
- **Writing samples to ask for:** a post that performed; a piece of copy they
  were proud of and didn't have to defend. One of each register if they have it.
- **Avoid-list prompts when they stall:** the LLM tells they already strip in
  every edit ("delve", "unlock", "elevate", "in today's fast-paced"); the
  em-dash habit; opening on a rhetorical question; the word their team teases
  them for.
- **The proof draft:** a two-line note declining a meeting — deliberately not a
  marketing asset, because a plain internal note is where a fake voice is most
  obvious.
- **Objectives note:** name the campaigns and the one metric that decides the
  quarter, plus what they've consciously parked, so the brief stops surfacing
  it.

## operations

- **Framing:** "Most of your writing is updates other people act on — status,
  handoffs, the note that stops a fire drill. Voice here means being
  unambiguous. Get the rules down once and every agent's output is something you
  can forward without rewriting."
- **Writing samples to ask for:** a status update people actually read; a
  handoff or process note that prevented a mess. (Also fine: revenue and
  marketing ops — same block, adapt the examples to their function in one line.)
- **Avoid-list prompts when they stall:** hedging they cut on every pass
  ("it seems", "we might want to consider"); the false urgency ("ASAP",
  "quick sync"); vague ownership ("someone should"); rounded numbers where
  they'd give the exact one.
- **The proof draft:** a two-line note declining a meeting. Their tell is
  usually the reason line — whether it names the actual conflict or waffles.
- **Objectives note:** rank the recurring work honestly, and use the "on hold /
  not now" section properly. Ops objectives are where that section earns its
  keep.

## neutral (role unset)

- **Framing:** "Three files, written once, read by every agent from here on:
  who you are, how you write, what matters now. Twenty minutes here makes
  everything after it better."
- **Writing samples to ask for:** any two short things they wrote and were
  happy with — an email, a message, a post. Sent, not drafted.
- **Avoid-list prompts when they stall:** the word you always cut on the last
  read; the opener you never use; whether you use exclamation points.
- **The proof draft:** a two-line note declining a meeting.
- **Objectives note:** three specific goals, specific enough that an agent
  could name their number one back to them.
