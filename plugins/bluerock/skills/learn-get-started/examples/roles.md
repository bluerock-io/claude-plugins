# Session 1 — role framing

Session 1 is setup, so the lesson is identical for every role. What role
changes here is the two-sentence scene-setting at the top and the "what you can
now do" line at the close — the *why this is worth ten minutes* framing.

Read the builder's `role` from `learning/progress.json` (values: `sales`,
`marketing`, `operations`). Use the matching block below. For `operations`,
use the sales block with a one-line "adapt this to your ops work." If role is
unset, use the neutral framing. Never change the steps.

## sales

- **Scene-setting:** "You're about to set up the project everything else builds
  in: a system that researches the accounts you care about, drafts in your
  voice, and keeps running the parts of your week you used to do by hand.
  Ten minutes of setup, and in the very next session it hands you a real
  account scorecard."
- **At the close:** "Next session, you point a team of agents at a real account
  you're chasing and get back a one-page scorecard you could send your manager."

## marketing

- **Scene-setting:** "You're about to set up the project everything else builds
  in: a system that reads your campaign numbers, drafts in your brand voice,
  and keeps running the parts of your week you used to assemble by hand.
  Ten minutes of setup, and in the very next session it hands you a real
  work product."
- **At the close:** "Next session, you point a team of agents at your own
  website and get back your core messaging doc — positioning, voice, and the
  phrases you actually use, on one page you could actually share."

## neutral (role unset)

- **Scene-setting:** "You're about to set up the project everything else builds
  in: your own cloud workspace, your own project, and a toolkit of agents that do
  real work in it. Ten minutes of setup, and in the very next session you get a
  real work product out of it."
- **At the close:** "Next session, you point a small team of agents at a real
  company and get back a one-page scorecard — your first real work product."
