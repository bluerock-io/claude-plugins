# Session 6 — role example blocks

The lesson is identical for everyone: run the seeded team, read why it's shaped
that way, name their own roles, build them, dispatch. Role changes the team shapes
offered in part two and the company suggested in part one.

Read `role` from `learning/progress.json` (`sales`, `marketing`, `operations`);
if unset, use the neutral block.

**Team shapes are prompts, not templates.** The input is supposed to be the job
*they* wrote down at the end of Session 5. Offer a shape only when they're stuck,
and always as "does your job split like this?" — never as the answer. And the
minimum is two roles: a gatherer and a maker. Three is not better.

## sales

- **Company for part one:** an account they're actually working, or the biggest
  logo in their patch. A company with a real public footprint gives the fullest
  dossier — worth saying, since a thin result on a stealth startup reads as a
  broken team.
- **Team shapes if they're stuck:**
  - *gatherer → writer:* one agent pulls everything on an account (news, people,
    filings), one drafts the outreach angle from it.
  - *gatherer → checker:* one assembles the pre-call brief, one checks it against
    what's already in the CRM notes so nothing is asked twice.
  - *two gatherers → one maker:* one on the company, one on the person, then a
    composer that writes the approach.
- **Least-privilege moment to point at:** the writer doesn't need the web. If it
  can browse, it will "improve" the sourced facts. Their own version of the
  composer lesson.
- **Real job for step 5:** a live account, this week.

## marketing

- **Company for part one:** a competitor. It's the highest-value first run for
  this role, and if they built a messaging doc in Session 2 they now have a
  baseline to compare against — the side-by-side promised in that session's
  close-out.
- **Team shapes if they're stuck:**
  - *gatherer → writer:* one reads a competitor's site and pricing, one drafts
    the positioning delta against their messaging doc.
  - *gatherer → maker → checker:* one collects the month's material, one drafts
    the newsletter, one checks every claim has a source.
  - *maker → critic:* one drafts a campaign brief, one attacks it — what's
    unclear, what's unmeasurable.
- **Least-privilege moment to point at:** the critic reads and writes only. A
  critic that can browse starts researching instead of critiquing.
- **Real job for step 5:** a live campaign or a competitor they're actually
  worried about.

## operations

- **Company for part one:** a vendor they're evaluating, or a tool they're about
  to renew. Same mechanism, and the output is immediately useful.
- **Team shapes if they're stuck:**
  - *gatherer → maker:* one collects the week's inputs from notes and files, one
    writes the status roll-up.
  - *maker → checker:* one drafts the process doc, one reads it as a new hire
    would and lists what's ambiguous. (This one is the most valuable checker in
    the program, and ops builders recognize it instantly.)
  - *gatherer → maker → checker:* the full shape, for a monthly report — collect,
    draft, verify the numbers are sourced.
- **Least-privilege moment to point at:** the checker gets `Read` and nothing
  that writes. A checker that can edit will quietly fix things instead of telling
  them what's wrong — and they lose the finding.
- **Real job for step 5:** this week's actual roll-up or a real process doc.
  (Revenue and marketing ops: same block, adapt in one line.)

## neutral (role unset)

- **Company for part one:** their own company, or one they're deciding whether to
  work with. Any real company with a public footprint works.
- **Team shapes if they're stuck:**
  - *gatherer → maker:* one collects, one drafts. The honest minimum, and it's
    enough.
  - *maker → checker:* one drafts, one reads it critically and lists what's off.
- **Least-privilege moment to point at:** whichever role doesn't need the web
  shouldn't have it. Ask "does this role need to look anything up, or does it work
  from what the other one produced?" — that question scopes the tools line every
  time.
- **Real job for step 5:** anything real from this week, however small.
